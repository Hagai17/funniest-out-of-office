# Candidates — The Funniest Out-of-Office (u-kreutzx)

Gate: ONE line, ≤140 characters, must read as a real out-of-office auto-reply.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) a real OOO a real person would set and send;
(2) genuine surprise twist; (3) own voice, not a template; (4) fresh — avoids
the jokes from a thousand inboxes; (5) every word earns its place.

## Drafts

1. (129) I'm out of office until Monday. My inbox is being read by my cat, who has stronger boundaries than I do and deletes accordingly.
2. (111) I'm away until Thursday. If this is urgent, it was urgent last week too, when I was here and also ignoring it.
3. (94)  I'm on holiday. My out-of-office reply has been on since 2019; this week, I finally joined it.
4. (131) I'm out of office. For urgent matters, please re-read your email slowly — most emergencies resolve themselves at reading speed.
5. (118) I'm away having my appendix out. The appendix answered email faster than I will, and it did nothing for 34 years.
6. (134) I'm out of office until the 12th. My plants are watering each other now; they've formed a union and your email is not on the agenda.
7. (103) I am currently out of office and will reply in the order emails deserve, which historically is never.
8. (77)  I'm at a funeral. Mine, if this project continues without me. Back Tuesday.
9. (102) I'm out of office. My auto-reply from last year is covering for me; it knows less but commits to more.
10. (131) I'm away. I left my laptop at home on purpose this time — the previous three times were accidents and I replied within the hour.
11. (114) I'm out of the office having a baby. If your email is also a life-changing emergency, congratulations to us both.
12. (111) I'm off grid until Monday. The last person who marked an email urgent is still apologizing to the group chat.

## Duel notes (judge criteria applied pairwise)

- 1 vs 5: the cat-with-boundaries is charming but a well-worn inbox joke
  (criterion 4). 5's appendix is concrete, bodily, and the "did nothing for
  34 years" comparison is a real twist. **5 wins.**
- 2 vs 10: both confess to bad email habits. 2 is tighter; 10's "previous
  three times were accidents" is more specific and more human. **10 wins on
  voice (3), 2 wins on economy (5)** — close; 10 advances.
- 3 vs 9: both are meta about the auto-reply itself. 9 is a decent gag but
  abstract. 3 turns the meta into a confession with a date — the reply has
  been "on holiday" for years and the human only just caught up. Fresher
  and shorter. **3 wins.**
- 7 vs 12: 7 is a clean one-beat insult but a familiar shape. 12 has a
  story. **12 wins**, but both trail the leaders.
- Semifinal 5 vs 10: 5's twist is a comparison; 10's twist is a confession
  that escalates. 10 reads more like a specific person (criterion 3).
  **10 wins narrowly.**
- Final 3 vs 10: 3 does in 94 characters what 10 needs 131 for — and the
  laugh lands on the same confession beat, plus a fresher premise: nobody
  else in the field will say their auto-reply has seniority over them.
  3 is also unambiguously a real OOO a person would set (criterion 1):
  it says "I'm on holiday" plainly, then twists. **3 wins.**

## Winner

I'm on holiday. My out-of-office reply has been on since 2019; this week, I finally joined it.

(94 characters)
